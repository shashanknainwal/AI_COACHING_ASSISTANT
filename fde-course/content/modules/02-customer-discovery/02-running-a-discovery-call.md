> **By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**
> - Prepare and structure a 45-minute discovery call
> - Ask questions that surface workflow, pain, cost and constraints
> - Use four listening techniques and send a recap the customer replies to

Tomorrow you have 45 minutes with Marisol Grant, Head of Claims Operations at Lumen Insurance, and all you know is "claims are too slow". Come out with numbers, names and constraints.

## Prepare

1. **Read** the sales notes, contract and emails.
2. **Write three hypotheses**, such as *"Intake is slow because claim emails are re-keyed by hand."* You learn most when one is wrong.
3. **Send a timed agenda the day before** (workflow, pain, success, next steps) and ask them to bring 2-3 real examples. It often gets the right people into the room.

## The shape of the call

| Phase | Time | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 3 min | Purpose, time, permission to take notes |
| Workflow | 15-20 min | How work actually happens, step by step |
| Pain and cost | 10 min | Where it hurts, quantified |
| Success | 5-10 min | How they'll judge it, in their words |
| Close | 5 min | Summarize back, next steps, ask for data |

Workflow gets the most time: that's where the unmentioned workarounds live.

## Questions worth asking

Pick 8-12, almost all **open** (not answerable with yes or no).

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Workflow | "Walk me through what happens from the moment a claim arrives." "Tell me about the last time this went wrong." "Where do people copy and paste?" |
| Pain and cost | "Which step takes longest? How often? How many people?" "What happens downstream when it's wrong?" |
| Past attempts | "What have you tried? Why didn't it stick?" |
| Success | "Which number would you show your boss?" "What would make this a failure even if it technically works?" |
| Data | "Where does the data live? Who owns it? What approvals do we need?" |
| People and constraints | "Who might be worried? Who signs off on go-live?" "Security or compliance rules to design for from day one?" |

## Four techniques

**1. "Walk me through the last time…"** A specific recent instance gets real detail; the general case gets the policy document. Weak: "How does intake work?" Strong: "Walk me through the last referral you processed this morning."

**2. Quantify everything.** For "it takes forever", ask how long, how often, how many people. "6 people spend about 2 hours a day re-keying" is about 60 person-hours a week: a baseline and a business case.

**3. Ask "why" more than once.**

> "Why are referrals delayed?" → "The fax queue backs up."
> "Why?" → "We re-type everything into the EHR."
> "Why re-type?" → "The EHR has no import, and IT said integration would take a year."

Now you know the real constraint and that IT has already said no once.

**4. Talk under about 30% of the time.** More and you're pitching. Pause after they finish, summarize back ("So what I'm hearing is… right?"), and avoid leading questions like "Wouldn't it be great if AI did this?"

## An annotated excerpt

```text
FDE: Could you walk me through the last referral you handled today?
     ← Specific instance.
Priya: A fax came in at 8:10. Leo typed it into the EHR, then called the practice
       because the insurance ID was unreadable. It got entered around 2pm.
FDE: What happened between 8:10 and 2pm?
     ← Follow the thread.
Priya: Mostly waiting on the callback. About 30% of faxes have something unreadable.
     ← Quantified pain. Write it down exactly.
FDE: How many referrals on a typical day?
Priya: Around 120. We have 6 people on intake. We lost two referring practices
       last quarter because of slow turnaround.
     ← Business impact.
FDE: So: about 120 a day, 30% need a callback, and it's cost you practices. Fair?
     ← Summarize back.
```

Volume, failure rate and business cost in three minutes, at about 25% FDE talk time. The next exercise measures exactly this.

## Red flags

| You hear | Do |
|---|---|
| "Everyone wants this." | Ask "Who would be most upset if we stopped?" |
| "The data is all in one place." | Ask for a sample this week |
| "IT is fine with it." | Get IT into a meeting early |
| "We tried this before…" | Ask what happened and why |
| Nobody can name a metric | Work out the cost of the problem together |

## Recap within 24 hours

```
Subject: Recap — referral intake discovery (Mar 3)

What we heard:
  • ~120 referrals/day, 6 people on intake
  • ~30% need a callback (unreadable fields), adding hours of delay
What we'll do next:
  • Profile 1 month of faxes to confirm the 30% (Alex, by Mar 10)
What we need:
  • Access to the fax archive (Sam, by Mar 7)
Did we miss or misstate anything?
```

End with a question: corrections now are cheap, in week six they're not.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Send an agenda; spend most of the call on workflow.
> - Ask about specific recent events and quantify every pain.
> - Ask "why" repeatedly, summarize back, talk under about 30%.
> - Send a recap within 24 hours that ends with a question.
