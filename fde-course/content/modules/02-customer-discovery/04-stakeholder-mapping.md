> **By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**
> - Place stakeholders on a power/interest grid and pick an engagement strategy for each
> - Test whether your sponsor and champion really are one
> - Spot and act on the stakeholder risks that kill engagements

Lumen Insurance's claims leadership is backing your project, but a CISO you haven't met can stop it with one email. You can build the right thing and still fail because the wrong person was surprised. A stakeholder map takes 20 minutes and saves weeks.

## The power/interest grid

Rate each person 1-5 on **influence** (budget, veto, control of data) and **interest** (how much the outcome affects their day).

| | Low interest (1-2) | High interest (3-5) |
|---|---|---|
| **High influence (3-5)** | **Keep satisfied:** short updates, consult before touching their area, every two weeks (e.g. CISO, CFO) | **Manage closely:** involve in decisions, no surprises, weekly (sponsor, champion) |
| **Low influence (1-2)** | **Monitor:** monthly note (adjacent teams) | **Keep informed:** weekly email or demo, gather feedback (end users) |

People move. A security lead with low interest becomes very interested the day you request production data. Revisit the map every couple of weeks.

## Test the roles

| Role | The test | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| **Sponsor** | Can they say yes to more budget or scope without asking anyone? If not, find who they'd ask. | Proof in their numbers, in two minutes of reading |
| **Champion** | Will they spend political capital: push for data access, defend you in meetings you're not in? Merely friendly and responsive is a **contact**. | Quick wins, responsiveness, credit |
| **Users** | Whose daily work changes? | Asked early, feedback reflected, time saved in week one |
| **Gatekeepers** | Security, IT, legal, procurement, data owners | Early involvement, data-flow docs, respect for their process |

## Stance

Add whether each person is a **supporter**, **neutral** (most users start here) or **skeptic**. A high-influence skeptic is the most important person on your map. Skeptics usually have a reason: a failed past project, fear for their team, data security, being left out. **Meet them early and one-on-one** and ask what would need to be true for them to be comfortable.

## Example: Lumen Insurance

| Name | Title | Influence | Interest | Stance | Role | Quadrant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joan Pierce | VP Claims | 5 | 4 | Supporter | Sponsor | Manage closely |
| Marcus Lee | Claims Ops Lead | 3 | 5 | Supporter | Champion | Manage closely |
| Ravi Shah | CISO | 5 | 2 | Skeptic | Gatekeeper | Keep satisfied |
| Adjuster team (12) | Claims Adjusters | 2 | 5 | Neutral | Users | Keep informed |
| Dee Ortiz | Finance BP | 2 | 2 | Neutral | — | Monitor |

- **Ravi is a high-influence skeptic**, the biggest risk. Book a meeting this week with a data-flow diagram and ask what he needs to approve.
- **Adjusters are neutral.** Sit with two for an hour each; make them co-designers of the review screen.
- **Only one champion.** Multi-thread: at least two strong relationships at each level, and a sponsor relationship that doesn't depend on the champion relaying messages.

## Politics without being political

Share the same facts with everyone. Never surprise a high-influence stakeholder in a group; preview bad news one-on-one. Give credit generously. Write decisions down.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Influence and interest set the strategy and cadence.
> - Test the sponsor (can they say yes to budget?) and champion (will they spend political capital?).
> - Meet high-influence skeptics early, one-on-one. Multi-thread.
