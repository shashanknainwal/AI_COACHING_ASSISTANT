questions:
  - q: "In this course, what makes a hiring claim 'Reported' rather than 'Anecdotal'?"
    options:
      - "It appears on several popular interview-prep websites"
      - "Several independent candidate accounts agree on it"
      - "A recruiter at the company mentioned it to one candidate"
      - "It has stayed unchanged in guides for more than a year"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Reported means several independent candidate accounts agree. One or two accounts is anecdotal; the company saying it makes it official. Prep sites repeating each other don't add accounts."
  - q: "Ten prep blogs repeat the same claim, and all of them trace back to one forum post. How should you label it?"
    options:
      - "Official, because it is so widely published"
      - "Reported, because there are ten separate sources"
      - "Anecdotal, because it's really one account"
      - "Unusable, because blogs never count as evidence"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Count independent origins, not repetitions. Ten copies of one post is still one account."
  - q: "Which four rounds keep recurring across the Anthropic, OpenAI and Perplexity loops?"
    options:
      - "Algorithm puzzles, ML trivia, a take-home and a reference call"
      - "Practical coding, LLM system design, a project deep dive, and values"
      - "A personality test, a group exercise, coding and a slide presentation"
      - "Probability, statistics, ML theory and a research talk on a paper"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Strip away the company names and these four come back every time. The course is built around them."
  - q: "Under Anthropic's official candidate AI guidance, how should you write your 'Why Anthropic?' application answer?"
    options:
      - "Write the first draft yourself, then let Claude help refine it"
      - "Ask Claude for a draft, then rewrite every sentence in your own voice"
      - "Ask Claude for an outline of points, then write the prose yourself"
      - "Avoid Claude entirely at every stage of the application process"
    answer: 0
    explain: "The guidance asks for your own first draft; refinement with Claude afterwards is fine. Starting from Claude's draft or outline means the thinking isn't yours, and the guidance encourages using Claude to prepare, so avoiding it entirely isn't required."
  - q: "You get a take-home assessment from Anthropic and nobody has said anything about AI. What does the official guidance say?"
    options:
      - "Use Claude freely, since it's their own product"
      - "Use Claude only for debugging, never for writing code"
      - "Use Claude, but disclose that use in a code comment"
      - "Do it without Claude unless told AI is allowed"
    answer: 3
    explain: "Take-homes and live interviews are AI-free unless Anthropic indicates otherwise. When in doubt, ask your recruiter."
  - q: "Which of these is an encouraged use of Claude in Anthropic's guidance?"
    options:
      - "Drafting answers about projects you didn't actually do"
      - "Keeping it open in a second window during a live interview"
      - "Working out how to quantify a project you really led"
      - "Having it write your solution in an online assessment"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Articulating real experience better is encouraged. Inventing experience, or using AI in an assessment without permission, is not."
  - q: "Anthropic's careers page says something about the coding tools used in technical interviews. What is the right way to use that?"
    options:
      - "Treat it as Official: practise in a plain browser editor such as CodeSignal or Colab, without your local setup"
      - "Ignore it, because only candidate reports describe what really happens"
      - "Assume you can paste in code generated by an AI assistant during the round"
      - "Assume it means there's no live coding at all for technical roles"
    answer: 0
    explain: "The company's own page is the Official source: it names live coding tools such as Colab and CodeSignal and says you can look things up. The one-problem, four-level shape of the assessment is still only Reported, and AI help stays off unless you're told otherwise."
  - q: "One candidate describes an AI-enabled coding screen in an OpenAI forward deployed engineering loop. How should you treat it?"
    options:
      - "As official OpenAI policy for every round"
      - "As anecdotal, and confirm each round with the recruiter"
      - "As reported, because it was published online"
      - "As false, because Anthropic doesn't allow AI either"
    answer: 1
    explain: "One account is anecdotal. No official OpenAI candidate AI policy was found, so ask the recruiter before each round."
  - q: "Which project is the best choice for your deep-dive round?"
    options:
      - "The team's flagship system, where a senior colleague made the key design calls"
      - "A polished tutorial project you followed step by step last month"
      - "A project you can't describe at all because of confidentiality"
      - "A smaller system where you made the calls, hit a failure and have numbers"
    answer: 3
    explain: "You'll be asked 'why?' repeatedly. Your own decisions, a defensible tradeoff, a failure and real numbers make a strong deep dive."
  - q: "Your prep guide says the coding assessment is 90 minutes, but your recruiter says 60. What do you do?"
    options:
      - "Prepare for 60 minutes"
      - "Prepare for 90 minutes, since the guide is based on many past accounts"
      - "Split the difference and prepare for about 75 minutes"
      - "Ignore both figures and practise without any clock at all"
    answer: 0
    explain: "Guides summarise past candidates. The recruiter knows your loop now. When they differ, the recruiter wins."
  - q: "You have only four weeks. What's the best way to compress the six-week plan?"
    options:
      - "Drop the values round, since it's only a conversation"
      - "Spend all four weeks on coding drills, since coding comes first"
      - "Merge weeks and cut depth, but practise all four rounds"
      - "Skip the mock loop and go straight to the real interviews"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Cut depth, not rounds. Each of the four rounds can end your loop on its own, so dropping any of them is a bad trade."
