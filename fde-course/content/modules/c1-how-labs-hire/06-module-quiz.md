title: "Module C1 Quiz"
type: quiz
minutes: 8
questions:
  - q: "In this course, what makes a hiring claim 'Reported' rather than 'Anecdotal'?"
    options:
      - "It appears on a popular prep website"
      - "Several independent candidate accounts agree on it"
      - "A recruiter mentioned it once"
      - "It has been true for more than a year"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Reported means several independent accounts agree. One or two accounts is anecdotal; the company saying it makes it official."
  - q: "Ten prep blogs repeat the same claim, and all of them trace back to one forum post. How should you label it?"
    options:
      - "Official"
      - "Reported, because there are ten sources"
      - "Anecdotal, because it's really one account"
      - "Unsupported, because blogs don't count"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Count independent origins, not repetitions. Ten copies of one post is still one account."
  - q: "Which four rounds keep recurring across the Anthropic, OpenAI and Perplexity loops?"
    options:
      - "Algorithm puzzles, trivia, a take-home and a reference call"
      - "Practical coding, LLM system design, a project deep dive, and motivation and values"
      - "A personality test, a group exercise, coding and a presentation"
      - "Math, statistics, ML theory and a research talk"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Strip away the company names and these four come back every time. The course is built around them."
  - q: "Under Anthropic's official candidate AI guidance, how should you write your 'Why Anthropic?' application answer?"
    options:
      - "Write the first draft yourself, then use Claude to help refine it"
      - "Ask Claude for a draft, then rewrite it in your voice"
      - "Ask Claude for an outline and fill it in yourself"
      - "Don't use Claude at all at any stage"
    answer: 0
    explain: "The guidance asks for your own first draft; refinement with Claude afterwards is fine. Starting from Claude's draft or outline means the thinking isn't yours."
  - q: "You get a take-home assessment from Anthropic and nobody has said anything about AI. What does the official guidance say?"
    options:
      - "Use Claude freely, since it's their product"
      - "Use Claude only for debugging"
      - "Use Claude but disclose it in a comment"
      - "Complete it without Claude unless they've said AI is allowed"
    answer: 3
    explain: "Take-homes and live interviews are AI-free unless Anthropic indicates otherwise. When in doubt, ask your recruiter."
  - q: "Which of these is an encouraged use of Claude in Anthropic's guidance?"
    options:
      - "Having it write answers about projects you didn't do"
      - "Running it in a second window during a live interview"
      - "Working out how to quantify the impact of a project you really led"
      - "Having it write your code in an online assessment"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Articulating real experience better is encouraged. Inventing experience, or using AI in an assessment without permission, is not."
  - q: "One candidate describes an AI-enabled coding screen in an OpenAI forward deployed engineering loop. How should you treat it?"
    options:
      - "As official OpenAI policy"
      - "As anecdotal: confirm the rules for each round with your recruiter"
      - "As reported, because it was published online"
      - "As false, because Anthropic doesn't allow AI"
    answer: 1
    explain: "One account is anecdotal. No official OpenAI candidate AI policy was found, so ask the recruiter before each round."
  - q: "Which project is the best choice for your deep-dive round?"
    options:
      - "The team's flagship system, where a senior colleague made the key design calls"
      - "A tutorial project you followed step by step last month"
      - "A project you can't describe at all because of confidentiality"
      - "A smaller system where you made the decisions, chose against an alternative, hit a failure and have real numbers"
    answer: 3
    explain: "You'll be asked 'why?' repeatedly. Own decisions, a defensible tradeoff, a failure and real numbers make a strong deep dive."
  - q: "Your prep guide says the coding assessment is 90 minutes, but your recruiter says 60. What do you do?"
    options:
      - "Prepare for 60 minutes: the recruiter is the most current, specific source"
      - "Prepare for 90 minutes: the guide is based on many accounts"
      - "Split the difference and prepare for 75"
      - "Ignore both and practise without a clock"
    answer: 0
    explain: "Guides summarise past candidates. The recruiter knows your loop now. When they differ, the recruiter wins."
  - q: "You have only four weeks. What's the best way to compress the six-week plan?"
    options:
      - "Drop the values round; it's only a conversation"
      - "Spend all four weeks on coding drills, since coding comes first"
      - "Merge weeks and cut depth, but still practise every one of the four rounds"
      - "Skip the mock loop and go straight to the real interview"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Cut depth, not rounds. Several guides call the values round the one that fails the most candidates at Anthropic (reported), so dropping any round is a bad trade."
