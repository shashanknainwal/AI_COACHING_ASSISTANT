> **By the end of this lesson** you'll be able to say exactly where AI help is allowed in a frontier-lab hiring process and where it isn't, and you'll have five concrete ways to use Claude for preparation that stay well inside the rules.

## Why this matters more than you think

You're preparing for jobs where AI is the product. It's tempting to assume the interviews are relaxed about AI. At Anthropic, the opposite is true for most of the process, and the rules are written down. Getting them wrong doesn't make you look modern. It makes you look like someone who didn't read the instructions, in a job that is mostly reading instructions carefully.

The good news: the rules are short, and they leave a lot of room to prepare well.

## Anthropic's official guidance

Anthropic publishes [guidance for candidates on using AI](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance) (last updated July 2025 when this lesson was checked on 2026-10-08). Everything in this section is **Official**. It's summarised here in our own words; read the page itself before you apply, because it may change.

| Stage | What the guidance says | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Application (resume, written answers) | Write the first draft yourself. Then Claude can help you refine it, for example by comparing your resume against the job description to find what to highlight. | **Official** |
| Take-home assessments | Do them without Claude, unless Anthropic tells you AI is allowed for that specific assessment. | **Official** |
| Interview preparation | Encouraged. Use Claude to research the company, practise your answers and prepare questions to ask. | **Official** |
| Live interviews | No AI assistance unless Anthropic says otherwise. In the page's own words: "This is all you–no AI assistance unless we indicate otherwise." | **Official** |
| Accommodations | If you need one, tell your recruiter early. | **Official** |

The guidance also draws the line with examples. Using Claude to put **real** experience into better words is fine, such as working out how to quantify the impact of a project you led. Using Claude to **invent** experience is not, such as having it write answers about work you never did. Using Claude during an assessment that hasn't allowed it, such as having it write your code, is not allowed either.

Three expectations sit on top of the rules:

1. **Use Claude as a collaborator**, thoughtfully, to strengthen your application and preparation.
2. **Be yourself.** AI should refine your ideas, not replace them.
3. **Be transparent**, to the same standard Anthropic says it applies to its own use of Claude.

The page also says how Anthropic uses Claude on its side of hiring (for things like drafting job descriptions and transcribing interviews), that it doesn't train Claude on candidate data, and that Claude doesn't make hiring decisions. That's **Official** too.

### The rule in one line

**Before the assessment and around your own words: Claude is welcome. Inside an assessment or interview: assume no AI unless told otherwise in writing.**

If you're unsure about a specific round, ask your recruiter. A question like "Is any AI tool allowed in the take-home?" is normal and costs you nothing.

## What "first draft yourself" actually means

This is the rule people bend without noticing. These all break it:

- Asking Claude for "a strong 'Why Anthropic?' answer" and then editing it into your voice.
- Pasting your resume and asking Claude to "write my application answers".
- Asking Claude for an outline and then filling it in.

The draft is the thinking. If Claude chose the structure and the points, the thinking isn't yours, however much you edit afterwards. A rough, short, slightly awkward draft that you wrote is the correct starting point. Then Claude can help.

## Five legitimate ways to prepare with Claude

### 1. Mock questions you didn't write

Ask Claude to play an interviewer for one round type and ask you original questions, one at a time, with follow-ups. For example: "Act as an interviewer for an LLM system design round. Ask me one original design question in the style of applied-AI interviews, then follow up on my weakest point." You get practice answering cold, which is the real skill.

Don't ask Claude for "the real questions Anthropic asks". It doesn't have them, and anything it produces will be a guess presented as fact.

### 2. Critique of your own first draft

Write your answer first. Then paste it and ask for critique, not a rewrite: "What's vague here? Which sentence would work for any company? Where am I claiming something without evidence?" Make the changes yourself. You'll keep your voice, and the next draft will be better without help.

### 3. Researching the lab

Use Claude to build a study guide: the lab's published essays and policies, its products, its stated values, recent engineering posts. Ask it to summarise, then go and read the originals yourself. Module C4 covers how to read these critically. Check any specific fact Claude gives you against the source; models can be out of date or wrong about recent events.

### 4. Practising explanations out loud

The project deep dive and the values round both reward clear explanation. Explain one of your projects to Claude and ask it to play a sceptical senior engineer: "Ask me why I made each decision. Push on the one that sounds weakest." Or explain a technical idea and ask it to tell you where a non-technical listener would get lost.

### 5. Quantifying what you really did

The guidance names this one as encouraged. Describe a real project with whatever numbers you have, and ask Claude what evidence would make the impact clearer and which numbers you should go and look up. Then look them up. Never let Claude fill in a number you don't know.

## What the other labs say

The picture outside Anthropic is much thinner. Label it carefully.

| Lab | What's known about AI in interviews | Label |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI | No official public candidate AI policy was found when this lesson was checked. One candidate account of the forward deployed engineering loop describes an AI-enabled coding screen. Third-party prep guides say the policy varies by team and by round. | **Anecdotal** (one account; guides are not candidate reports) |
| Perplexity | No public policy on AI use in interviews was found. | Unknown |

"Anecdotal" here means you shouldn't plan around it. If you're interviewing at OpenAI, the only reliable answer is the one your recruiter gives you for each round. Practise both ways: unaided, and with an assistant, so neither format throws you.

## A simple test before every use

Before you open Claude during your application or interview process, ask yourself:

1. **Is this an assessment or a live interview?** If yes, and nobody told you AI is allowed, close it.
2. **Am I starting from my own words?** If not, write them first.
3. **Would I be comfortable telling the interviewer exactly how I used it?** If not, don't do it.

If all three answers are good, use Claude as much as you like. It's one of the best practice partners you'll have.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Anthropic's rules are official and short: own first drafts, no AI in take-homes or live interviews unless told otherwise, and AI-assisted preparation is encouraged.
> - Use Claude to refine your real experience, never to invent it, and ask for critique rather than rewrites.
> - The best prep uses: mock questions, critique of your drafts, researching the lab, practising explanations, and finding the numbers behind your impact.
> - Other labs' AI rules are not officially published; the OpenAI "AI-enabled screen" is one account. Ask your recruiter about each round.
