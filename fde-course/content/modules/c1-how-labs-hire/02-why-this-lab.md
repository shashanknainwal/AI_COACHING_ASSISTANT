Your application's written answer is the first thing a reader at the lab sees, and reports suggest they read it closely. Anthropic's own guidance is to **write the first draft yourself** and only then use AI to refine it. This exercise follows the same rule: the draft is yours.

## The task

Pick one lab you'd genuinely apply to. In 200–400 words, answer: **why do you want to work there, in this role?**

A strong answer usually does three things in a few short paragraphs:

1. **Something specific about the lab.** A position it has taken, a product decision, an essay, a research direction. Say what it is and why it matters to you.
2. **Something specific about you.** A project or customer story that shows you already care about the same thing. Use a detail only you would know.
3. **Why now, and why this role.** What you'd do in your first months, in plain words.

## What to avoid

- **The swap test.** If you could change the lab's name and the answer still works, it isn't specific enough.
- **Adjective soup.** "Passionate about cutting-edge AI" says nothing. A number or a name says something.
- **Flattery.** Interviewers at mission-driven labs respect candidates who can hold a real question about the mission. You don't have to agree with everything to want to work there.

Submit for review when you're done. You'll get a score per rubric line, the strongest thing in your draft, and the one change to make first. Revise and resubmit as often as you like.
