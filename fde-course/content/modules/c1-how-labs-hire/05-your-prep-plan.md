> **By the end of this lesson** you'll have a 4–8 week prep plan that covers all four recurring rounds, maps each one to the modules in this course, picks the right project for your deep dive, and uses recruiter calls to confirm what you're actually preparing for.

## Start from the four rounds

Lesson 1 showed that every loop, at every lab we looked at, keeps coming back to four rounds. Your plan should give each one real hours, not just the one you enjoy.

| Round | What it tests | Where this course trains it |
|---|---|---|
| Practical coding | Building and extending working Python fast, without a mess | C3 (progressive drills), plus C2 for the LLM facts you'll be asked to code against |
| LLM system design | Requirements, architecture, evals, failure modes, cost | C2 (fundamentals), then your track's design module: E6 for engineers, A3 and A6 for architects, the tools, evals and production modules for FDEs |
| Project deep dive | Defending decisions you made, under follow-ups | Your own project, or your track's capstone |
| Motivation and values | Why this lab, how you behave when the right thing is expensive | C1 (your written answer), C4 (reading the labs), C5 (values and behavioural) |

The labels on these rounds come from Lesson 1: the four-round shape is **Reported** at Anthropic, OpenAI and Perplexity. The exact mix for your role is something only your recruiter can confirm.

## How long the process takes

Plan your prep against the process, not against a fixed date you picked.

| Fact | Label |
|---|---|
| At Anthropic, references and team matching after the onsite can add two to four weeks or more. | Reported |
| End-to-end, third-party guides give anywhere from about three weeks to three months for Anthropic, with team matching the biggest variable. Sources disagree a lot. | Anecdotal (inconsistent guides) |
| Passing the onsite isn't an offer at Anthropic: candidates describe a team-matching stage that can run long with little news. | Anecdotal (a handful of forum accounts) |
| How Anthropic sets levels | No reliable public source. Ask your recruiter. |

Two practical consequences. First, **don't apply before you've started preparing**: at Anthropic the online assessment is reported to come straight after the recruiter screen, and you want C3's drills behind you by then. Ask your recruiter how much time you'll get. Second, **expect a quiet stretch at the end**. Silence during team matching isn't necessarily a rejection. Keep your skills warm and keep your other processes moving.

## The plan: six weeks, adjustable to four or eight

About 8–10 hours a week. Each week lists the main focus; keep a small amount of coding practice running every week, because the coding screen usually comes first.

| Week | Focus | Modules | Output by the end of the week |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The map and your story | C1 | Your "Why this lab?" draft (your own first draft, then Claude critique). Your deep-dive project chosen. Questions for the recruiter written down. |
| 2 | Fundamentals and the format | C2, C3 lesson 1 | You can explain tokens, context, tool use, caching and cost math out loud without notes. One untimed drill done. |
| 3 | Coding under the clock | C3 drills, timed, tutor off | Two timed drills completed. A list of the spec details you misread. |
| 4 | Design | Your track's design module | Two full design answers written against the rubric. One talked through out loud. |
| 5 | The lab and the values round | C4, C5 | One written critique of a lab essay. One mock values interview scored. Three STAR stories you can tell in two minutes each. |
| 6 | Full loop | Your track's mock loop | One full mock loop end to end. Fix the weakest round, then re-run just that round. |

**Four weeks?** Merge weeks 1–2 and weeks 4–5, and do the mock loop in week 4. Cut depth, not rounds: one drill, one design answer and one values mock are worth more than three of any single one.

**Eight weeks?** Add a week of track depth after week 2 (for example E2–E4 for engineers, A4–A5 for architects, the integrations and agents modules for FDEs), and a second mock loop in week 8.

**Your track matters for weeks 4 and 6.** Applied AI Engineers lean into E1–E6 and finish with E7. Architects lean into A1–A6 and finish with A7, where case presentation and customer role-play replace some of the coding. FDEs lean into the customer-engagement modules and use the capstone as their deep-dive project.

## Picking your deep-dive project

The project deep dive is **Reported** at all three labs. You choose the project, so choose well. A good one has:

1. **Decisions you made yourself.** You'll be asked "why?" five times in a row. If someone else made the key calls, pick a different project.
2. **A tradeoff you can defend.** Something you chose against a reasonable alternative, and what it cost you.
3. **Something that went wrong.** A failure, a wrong assumption, an incident. How you found it and what you changed says more than the success.
4. **Numbers.** Latency, cost, accuracy, users, hours saved. Use real ones; go and look them up now if you don't have them.
5. **Relevance.** An LLM system is ideal for applied roles, but a strong non-AI project beats a weak AI one. Customer-facing work is a plus for FDE and architect roles.

If nothing you've built fits, the capstone in your track is designed to. Build it properly and treat it as yours.

Mind your confidentiality agreements. You can describe the shape of work you did for an employer or customer without naming them or showing their code. Prepare that version in advance.

## Using recruiter calls to confirm the format

Your recruiter is the only source on your specific loop, and asking good questions makes you look prepared, not anxious. Ask in the first call and again before each stage:

- "What are the stages for this role, and roughly how long does each take?"
- "What language and environment is the coding assessment in? Is it one problem or several?"
- "Is any AI tool allowed in the take-home or the coding rounds?"
- "What should I prepare for the project deep dive? Do I present slides, or talk through it?"
- "Is the system design round general, or focused on LLM systems?"
- "Which level is this role considered for, and how is level decided?"

Write the answers down. Where they differ from what you read here or anywhere else, **the recruiter wins**: that's the most current, most specific source you'll get. Then adjust the plan.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Plan across all four rounds every week, with coding practice running throughout because it usually comes first.
> - Six weeks at 8–10 hours a week is a solid default; compress to four by merging weeks, stretch to eight by adding track depth and a second mock loop.
> - Pick a deep-dive project with your own decisions, a defensible tradeoff, a failure and real numbers.
> - Timelines after the onsite are long and variable (reported and anecdotal); levels aren't publicly documented.
> - Ask the recruiter about format, AI rules and level at every stage, and let their answers override any guide.
