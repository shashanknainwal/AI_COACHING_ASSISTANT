These are the two stories that values interviewers reach for most often, and the two that candidates most often get wrong. Writing them down first makes them much easier to tell out loud later in this module.

## The task

Write two short stories, 150–300 words each, in STAR shape (see the previous lesson).

1. **A disagreement on principle.** You and someone you respected disagreed about what was *right*: about a user's privacy, about shipping something you thought wasn't ready, about how a colleague was treated. A disagreement about which framework to use doesn't count unless something more was at stake.
2. **A mistake that affected other people.** Your error cost someone else time, money, trust or sleep. Say what the cost was.

## What strong looks like

- One real event per story. Roles, not names. No confidential customer details.
- Most of your words on what **you** did and what **happened**.
- A real cost or loose end. Not everything has to end well.
- A last line on what you do differently now.

## Common traps

- **"We".** If the reader can't tell what you personally did, the story doesn't count.
- **The humble-brag.** "I took on too much because I care" isn't a mistake. Pick one you'd rather not talk about.
- **The strawman.** If the other person in your disagreement story sounds foolish, the reader will doubt the story. Describe their best argument.
