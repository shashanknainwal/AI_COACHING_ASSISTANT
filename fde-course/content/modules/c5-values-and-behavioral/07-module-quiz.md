  - q: "What do several career guides say about Anthropic's culture or values interview?"
    options:
      - "It's a formality once you pass the coding rounds"
      - "It's the round that fails the most candidates"
      - "It's only used for senior roles"
      - "Anthropic publishes the scoring rubric for it"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Several third-party guides report it as the round that fails the most candidates. That's Reported, not Official: Anthropic doesn't publish pass rates or its rubric."
  - q: "In a strong STAR answer, where should most of your words go?"
    options:
      - "Situation and Task, so the interviewer has full context"
      - "An even split across all four parts"
      - "Action and Result"
      - "Task and Result"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Keep context to a sentence or two. Spend roughly half on what you did and a third on what happened, including what you'd change."
  - q: "An interviewer asks about a mistake. Which answer is the weakest?"
    options:
      - "I pushed a config change without a staged rollout, and it took down a customer's integration for three hours."
      - "I misread a customer's requirements, and they lost a week of work before I caught it."
      - "I promised a deadline I hadn't checked with the team, and two people worked a weekend to hit it."
      - "My biggest mistake is that I care too much and take on too much work."
    answer: 3
    explain: "That's a humble-brag disguised as a mistake. Real mistakes have a real cost to someone. The other three do."
  - q: "Asked 'Would you prioritize the mission over the future share price?', which answer best fits the four-move framework?"
    options:
      - "Yes, I'd take the mission. I've seen a company ship something harmful to hit a target. I'd feel it financially, though, and if 'mission' became an excuse to dodge accountability I'd push back."
      - "Absolutely. I agree with everything your company stands for."
      - "Honestly, the money is what matters most to me at this stage."
      - "That depends on so many factors that I can't really say."
    answer: 0
    explain: "It gives a position, a reason from real life, an honest tension, and a limit. The others flatter, dodge, or give no reasoning."
  - q: "You want to disagree with a position a lab has published. What should you do first?"
    options:
      - "State their position fairly, in a way its authors would accept"
      - "Say you're skeptical of the whole approach"
      - "Avoid the topic; disagreement always costs points"
      - "Quote the essay back to show you've read it"
    answer: 0
    explain: "Steelman first, then name one specific point, give your reason, and say what would change your mind."
  - q: "Which answer style is listed as one of the three that fail reliably in mission questions?"
    options:
      - "Admitting you're unsure about part of the lab's approach"
      - "Fatalism, such as 'we're probably all doomed, but I want to help'"
      - "Naming one specific AI risk you've read about, with the source"
      - "Mentioning your own financial situation"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Flattery, doom and dismissal fail. Concern about specific risks is legitimate; fatalism isn't. Honesty about uncertainty or money is a strength."
  - q: "In a project deep dive, the interviewer pushes back: 'Why not just use a managed queue instead?' What's the best response?"
    options:
      - "Agree immediately so you don't seem defensive"
      - "Say it was the team's decision, not yours"
      - "Explain the reasons for your choice and what it cost, or change your view if their point reveals something you missed"
      - "Change the subject to a different part of the project"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Pushback tests whether you reasoned about the decision. Defend it with reasons, or update for a reason. Folding with no reason scores poorly."
  - q: "Which use of AI fits Anthropic's official candidate AI guidance for preparing behavioral stories?"
    options:
      - "Have Claude write your stories, then memorize them"
      - "Use Claude live during the interview to suggest answers"
      - "Ask Claude to invent a more impressive version of a weak story"
      - "Write your story notes yourself, then practice with Claude asking you follow-up questions"
    answer: 3
    explain: "The guidance (Official) says write first drafts yourself, and encourages using Claude to prepare and practice. No AI in live interviews unless permitted."
