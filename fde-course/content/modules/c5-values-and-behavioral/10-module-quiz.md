questions:
  - q: "Several career guides make the same claim about how hard Anthropic's values round is, and you can't trace it to any candidate account. How should you treat the claim?"
    options:
      - "As Official, because several independent guides all agree on it"
      - "As Reported, because it has been published in more than one place"
      - "As Anecdotal, while still preparing the round seriously"
      - "As false, because Anthropic itself has never confirmed it in writing"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Guides aren't candidate reports and may copy each other, and Anthropic publishes no pass rates. Count independent accounts, not repetitions. The round still deserves real preparation."
  - q: "In a strong STAR answer, where should most of your words go?"
    options:
      - "Situation and Task, so the interviewer has full context first"
      - "An even split across all four parts, for balance"
      - "Action and Result"
      - "Task and Result, since those show what was at stake"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Keep context to a sentence or two. Spend roughly half on what you did and a third on what happened, including what you'd change."
  - q: "An interviewer asks about a mistake. Which answer is the weakest?"
    options:
      - "I pushed a config change without a staged rollout and took down a customer's integration for three hours."
      - "I misread a customer's requirements, and they lost a week of work before I caught it."
      - "I promised a deadline I hadn't checked with the team, and two people worked a weekend."
      - "My biggest mistake is that I care too much."
    answer: 3
    explain: "That's a humble-brag disguised as a mistake. Real mistakes have a real cost to someone. The other three do."
  - q: "Asked 'Would you prioritize the mission over the future share price?', which answer best fits the four-move framework?"
    options:
      - "Yes: I've seen a harmful launch made to hit a target. I'd feel it financially, and I'd push back if 'mission' became an excuse."
      - "Absolutely, without any hesitation at all. I agree with everything your company stands for, and I always have done."
      - "Honestly, the money is what matters most to me at this stage of my career, so I'd pick the share price."
      - "That depends on so many different factors, timelines and circumstances that I really can't say either way, sorry."
    answer: 0
    explain: "It gives a position, a reason from real life, an honest tension, and a limit. The others flatter, dodge, or give no reasoning."
  - q: "You want to disagree with a position a lab has published. What should you do first?"
    options:
      - "State their position fairly"
      - "Say up front that you're skeptical of the whole approach"
      - "Avoid the topic entirely, since disagreement always costs points"
      - "Quote long passages of the essay to prove you've read it"
    answer: 0
    explain: "Steelman first, in a way the authors would accept. Then name one specific point, give your reason, and say what would change your mind."
  - q: "Which answer style is one of the three that fail reliably in mission questions?"
    options:
      - "Admitting you're unsure about part of the lab's approach"
      - "Fatalism: 'we're probably all doomed, but I want to help'"
      - "Naming one specific AI risk you've read about, with its source"
      - "Mentioning your own financial situation as part of the trade-off"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Flattery, doom and dismissal fail. Concern about specific risks is legitimate; fatalism isn't. Honesty about uncertainty or money is a strength."
  - q: "In a project deep dive, the interviewer pushes back: 'Why not just use a managed queue instead?' What's the best response?"
    options:
      - "Agree straight away, so you don't come across as defensive"
      - "Explain the reasons and the cost, or update if they've found a gap"
      - "Say it was the team's decision rather than yours, so you can't say"
      - "Move the conversation to a different, stronger part of the project"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Pushback tests whether you reasoned about the decision. Defend it with reasons, or update for a reason. Folding with no reason scores poorly."
  - q: "Which is the strongest ending for a two-minute 'tell me about yourself' answer?"
    options:
      - "A summary of where you went to university and your first job"
      - "A list of every programming language and framework you know"
      - "A pause, so the interviewer can choose the next question"
      - "Why this role, at this company, now"
    answer: 3
    explain: "The pitch runs now, how you got here, one proof story, then why you're in this conversation. Ending there saves the interviewer from having to ask."
  - q: "Anthropic's careers page lists seven principles. How should you use them in a values round?"
    options:
      - "Recite each principle's name as you tell each story, so the interviewer can score it easily"
      - "Ignore them, since no public source says interviewers use them"
      - "Use them to check your stories' coverage, and let the stories speak"
      - "Rewrite your stories so every one of them matches a principle"
    answer: 2
    explain: "The list is Official, but how interviewers use it isn't published. Map your stories against it to find gaps, never bend a story to fit, and don't name-drop the headings."
  - q: "In an LLM project deep dive, you're asked how you measured your system's hallucination rate. Which answer is strongest?"
    options:
      - "It basically never hallucinates; the team checked it a lot by hand"
      - "A defined error, a validated grounding check, 300 cases, a range"
      - "We told the model in the system prompt not to make things up"
      - "We switched to the largest model, which hallucinates the least"
    answer: 1
    explain: "A strong answer defines what counts as a hallucination, says how it was judged and checked against humans, gives the sample size, and is honest about uncertainty."
  - q: "Which use of AI fits Anthropic's official candidate AI guidance for preparing behavioral stories?"
    options:
      - "Have Claude write polished stories for you, then memorise them"
      - "Use Claude live during the interview to suggest better answers"
      - "Ask Claude to invent a more impressive version of a weak story"
      - "Write notes yourself, then practise with Claude asking questions"
    answer: 3
    explain: "The guidance (Official) says write first drafts yourself, and encourages using Claude to prepare and practise. No AI in live interviews unless permitted."
