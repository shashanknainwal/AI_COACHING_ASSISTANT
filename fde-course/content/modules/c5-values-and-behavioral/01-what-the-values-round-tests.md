> **By the end of this lesson** you'll know what candidates report about the values and behavioral rounds at Anthropic, OpenAI and Perplexity, and what interviewers are actually listening for when they ask about your motivation and your past.

## Why this round deserves real preparation

Most engineers prepare for coding for weeks and for the values round for an evening. That's backwards at a mission-driven lab. Several interview guides single out the culture or values interview at Anthropic as the round that fails the most candidates (**Reported**: [Yale SOM career guide](https://cdo.som.yale.edu/blog/2026/05/18/get-a-job-at-anthropic-interview-process-and-top-questions/), plus other third-party guides). Strong technical candidates fail it because they treat it as small talk.

The round isn't a test of whether you hold the "right" opinions. It's a test of whether your answers are specific, honest and consistent with how you've actually behaved.

## Anthropic: the culture interview

What's been reported:

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The culture interview is part of the first onsite loop, alongside coding and system design. | Reported | Third-party loop guides, e.g. [Yale SOM](https://cdo.som.yale.edu/blog/2026/05/18/get-a-job-at-anthropic-interview-process-and-top-questions/) |
| It is run by an employee nominated for the role, not necessarily someone on your future team. | Reported | [Axios AI+ newsletter](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-ai-plus-10f46cb9-9c35-4d55-b72c-498c853ea10b.html) |
| Candidates have been asked whether they'd prioritize the mission over the company's future share price. | Reported | [Axios AI+](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-ai-plus-10f46cb9-9c35-4d55-b72c-498c853ea10b.html), career guides |
| Candidates have been asked to describe someone they respect but disagree with on values. | Reported | [Axios AI+](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-ai-plus-10f46cb9-9c35-4d55-b72c-498c853ea10b.html), career guides |
| It's the round that fails the most candidates. | Reported | Several career guides; Anthropic hasn't published pass rates |

Two things follow. First, a nominated interviewer is often someone who cares a lot about the culture, so polished corporate answers land badly. Second, both reported questions have no correct answer. The mission question checks whether you've thought about the tradeoff at all. The disagreement question checks whether you can describe a person you disagree with fairly, which is hard to fake.

Anthropic doesn't publish its values rubric. Anything more specific you read online about "what they score" is a guess.

## OpenAI: behavioral and the project deep dive

For forward deployed and solutions roles, candidates describe (**Reported**):

- A recruiter screen that covers motivation, your views on AI and why you want customer-facing work.
- A **project deep dive** in the onsite, where you present something you built and defend it under follow-ups. Lesson 06 of this module drills it.
- Assessment of how you scope ambiguous problems and explain technical work to non-technical people. Behavioral questions often come in this form: "tell me about a time a customer wanted something you thought was wrong."

Sources: [Exponent guide](https://www.tryexponent.com/guides/openai-forward-deployed-engineer-interview), [IGotAnOffer guide](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/openai-forward-deployed-engineer-interview).

## Perplexity: the hiring-manager round

Candidates describe a **hiring-manager deep dive** in the onsite (**Reported**), with a technical founder interview for senior roles (**Reported**). The deep dive mixes "walk me through your project" with behavioral questions about ownership, speed and how you handle ambiguity. Sources: [Design Gurus](https://www.designgurus.io/answers/detail/what-is-the-perplexity-interview-process-like-round-by-round), [Interview Query](https://www.interviewquery.com/prep-guides/perplexity-ai-software-engineer).

Public detail on specific behavioral questions at Perplexity is thin. Prepare the general story bank in lesson 02 rather than chasing question lists.

## What interviewers are actually listening for

Across all three labs, the same four signals come up again and again.

### 1. Specificity

"I always try to communicate early" is a claim. "In March, I told our VP the launch would slip two weeks, in writing, before my manager had signed off" is evidence. Interviewers follow up on vague answers until they reach a real situation. If you don't have one, the follow-ups expose it.

### 2. Honesty

Real stories have loose ends: a decision you're still unsure about, a colleague who was partly right, a result that was mixed. Answers where you were right, everyone agreed eventually and the metrics went up read as rehearsed. Saying "I don't know" or "I got that wrong" costs less than you think.

### 3. Judgment under tradeoffs

Values questions are usually tradeoff questions in disguise: speed versus safety, customer versus principle, your team versus the company. Strong answers name both sides, say what you chose, and say what it cost. Weak answers pretend there was no tension.

### 4. Motivation beyond money and hype

"AI is the most exciting field right now" is true for everyone in the room. Interviewers want to hear what you specifically want to do, why at this lab, and whether you'd still want it if the job got harder or the equity got smaller. The share-price question is a direct probe of this.

## Common ways strong candidates fail

| Failure | What it sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hypotheticals | "I would raise it with my manager." | Answer with a real past event, then say what you'd do now. |
| "We" stories | "We decided to roll back." | Say what *you* did, even if it was small. |
| Flattery | "I agree with everything your company stands for." | Name one thing you've thought hard about, including a question you still have. |
| Humble-brag mistakes | "My biggest mistake is working too hard." | Pick a mistake that cost someone something. |
| Scripted answers | The same polished paragraph regardless of the follow-up. | Prepare stories, not scripts. Answer the follow-up that was asked. |

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - At Anthropic, the culture round is reported to fail more candidates than any other. Prepare it like a technical round.
> - Reported questions (mission versus share price, someone you respect but disagree with) have no right answer. They test honesty and depth of thought.
> - OpenAI and Perplexity mix behavioral questions into project deep dives and hiring-manager rounds.
> - Interviewers listen for specificity, honesty, judgment under tradeoffs, and motivation beyond money and hype.
