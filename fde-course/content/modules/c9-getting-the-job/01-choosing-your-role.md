> **By the end of this lesson** you'll be able to score your own experience against the three applied roles, pick the one your evidence supports best, and say in one sentence why you chose it over the other two.

## Why the choice comes first

Modules C1 to C5 prepared you for the loop. This module is about everything around it: picking the role, getting the call, handling the recruiter, the take-home, the offer and the negotiation.

It starts with the role because that choice shapes everything after it. Your resume bullets, your outreach note, your "Why us?" answer and your recruiter pitch all point at one job. A candidate who applies to all three applied roles with the same resume tells the reader they haven't decided what they're good at.

Your coach for this module is **Hana Ostrowski**, a fictional career coach at the fictional Ossian Careers. She'll frame the exercises. The advice is ours, not hers.

## A fourth label: General

The course labels hiring claims **Official**, **Reported** or **Anecdotal** (module C1). Much of this module is different: it's general hiring practice that isn't specific to any lab, such as how resume screens tend to work or how equity usually vests. We label that **General**. It means "widely shared industry practice and our judgement, not a fact about one company's process". When a lab's own practice differs, the lab wins, and your recruiter is the most current source.

## Score yourself

Score each statement honestly. **0** = no evidence. **1** = some evidence (a side project, a course, one occasion). **2** = strong evidence you could talk about for five minutes with a number in it.

| # | Statement | Engineer | Architect | FDE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I've built and shipped an LLM feature that real users touched. | x2 | x1 | x2 |
| 2 | I've built an eval set and used it to make a decision. | x2 | x1 | x1 |
| 3 | I've written production code in two or more languages. | x1 | | x2 |
| 4 | I've worked inside someone else's codebase or infrastructure under time pressure. | x1 | | x2 |
| 5 | I've designed a system across another team's security, data or compliance constraints. | x1 | x2 | x1 |
| 6 | I've presented a technical recommendation to executives or a buying committee. | | x2 | |
| 7 | I've run discovery with a customer and turned it into a written scope. | x1 | x2 | x1 |
| 8 | I've helped another team's engineers succeed with a tool or platform I knew well. | x2 | x1 | x1 |
| 9 | I've been on call for, or debugged, a system in production. | x1 | | x2 |
| 10 | I can travel a quarter to half of the time for the next few years. | | | x2 |

Multiply each score by the weight in each column and add up the columns. A blank means 0. The maximum is 22 for Engineer, 18 for Architect and 28 for FDE, so compare **percentages**, not raw totals.

The weights are our judgement (**General**), built from the postings in C1. They are not a lab's scoring model. Use the result as a conversation with yourself, not a verdict.

### Reading your result

- **One role clearly ahead (10+ points of percentage):** apply for it. Use the other two as fallbacks only if a recruiter suggests them.
- **Two roles close:** choose on the statement you'd most like to be true in two years. That's your motivation answer, and interviewers test motivation in every loop (C1).
- **Row 10 scored 0:** be honest with yourself about the FDE role. The travel figure is in the posting we read. It isn't a detail you negotiate away in the offer.
- **Rows 1 and 2 both 0:** fix that before you apply for any of them. The capstone (module C8) and the E-track exercises exist for exactly this.

## Say why in one sentence

Write one sentence that names the role and the evidence that points to it. For example (a fictional candidate; write your own):

> "Forward Deployed Engineer, because the best work I've done is shipping a document pipeline inside a customer's AWS account in three weeks, and I'd like that to be the whole job."

If you can't write the sentence without "I'm passionate about", go back to the table. The sentence will open your recruiter pitch (lesson 8) and anchor your "Why us?" answer (lesson 7).

## Then decode the posting

C1 lesson 1 showed the manual version: list every requirement, write evidence next to it, mark green, amber or red. The next lesson turns that into a small program, so you can run it against every posting you consider and see which one your evidence fits, and what to work on first.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Choose one role before you write anything. Every artefact in your application points at it.
> - **General** means widely shared hiring practice and our judgement, not a lab's process. The lab and your recruiter override it.
> - Score yourself against the ten statements, compare percentages, and read the travel and evidence rows honestly.
> - Write one sentence: the role, plus the evidence that points to it. You'll reuse it in your pitch and your written answers.
