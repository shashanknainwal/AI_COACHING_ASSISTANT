## Referrals: what they do and don't do

Labs don't publish how referrals are weighted, so this section is **General**: common practice in tech hiring.

- **A referral usually gets your application a closer look,** often from a recruiter rather than a first-pass screen. It rarely changes the loop itself. You still pass the same rounds.
- **The best referral comes from someone who knows your work.** A former colleague who can say "I worked with them for two years" is worth more than a stranger who clicked a button.
- **A stranger can't honestly vouch for you.** Asking one for a referral puts them in an awkward spot. Ask for advice or a short conversation instead, and let a referral follow only if they offer.
- **Never ask an employee for interview questions.** Candidates agree to confidentiality; employees have obligations too. Asking puts them in a bad position and tells them something about you. Ask about the work instead.

We found no public, official description of a referral programme at Anthropic, OpenAI or Perplexity. If an employee offers to refer you, they'll know their own process.

## Who to contact, in order

1. **People who've worked with you** and now work at the company or know someone there.
2. **Second-degree connections** a mutual contact can introduce you to. Ask the mutual contact first; give them a forwardable paragraph.
3. **People whose public work you've engaged with:** a talk, a post, an open-source project. Mention the specific thing.
4. **Recruiters for the role,** with a three-line note and the posting link. Recruiters expect these.

## What a good note looks like

Five parts, under about 150 words:

| Part | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Why you** | One honest line about the connection or their work | 1 sentence |
| **The role** | The exact title and a link | 1 sentence |
| **Proof** | One result with a number or a link, relevant to the role | 1–2 sentences |
| **The ask** | One small thing they can say yes or no to | 1 sentence |
| **The blurb** | Two or three sentences they could forward unchanged | Optional, but powerful |

Then a polite close. Make "no" easy: "no worries if not" removes the pressure and usually raises the reply rate (**General**).

**Following up:** one short follow-up after about a week is normal. After that, let it go (**General**).

**Your first draft is yours.** Anthropic's guidance asks for your own first drafts on application materials (**Official**). An outreach note isn't strictly part of the application, but the same standard is the safe one: people can tell a template.
