## Why look wider

This course covers Anthropic in depth, with OpenAI and Perplexity as comparisons. But forward deployed and applied AI roles now exist at many companies: large AI labs, enterprise software companies and AI application startups. Applying more widely gives you practice loops, competing offers (lessons 11–12) and more chances at the work you chose in lesson 1.

A warning first. We have **not** researched these companies' interview processes to the standard of C1, so this lesson makes no claims about their loops. What follows is limited to what their job listings show and what transfers. Listings were seen on 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-11, some through job-board mirrors that can lag or be wrong. Check each company's own careers site.

## What transfers

The four rounds from C1 (practical coding, LLM system design, a project deep dive, motivation and values) are reported at all three labs we cover. We don't know whether other companies use the same shape, but the **skills** underneath them are what every applied or forward deployed posting asks for (**General**, based on reading the listings above):

| Skill | Where you trained it | Why it transfers |
|---|---|---|
| Practical, extend-without-rewriting coding | C3 drills | Customer code changes constantly; every posting asks for strong coding |
| Discovery and scoping | FDE modules 1–2, A1 | Every forward deployed role starts with an unclear customer problem |
| Integrations and messy data | FDE modules 3–5 | The core of shipping inside someone else's systems |
| Evals and honest measurement | C2, E4, module 8 | Model-agnostic; works with any provider's models |
| LLM system design | E6, A3, A6 | The patterns (retrieval, agents, caching, fallbacks) are provider-neutral |
| Customer communication | A5, field drills | Same skill in every customer-facing role |
| A defensible project | C8 capstone | Every deep dive needs one |

What doesn't transfer directly:

- **Provider-specific API details.** This course teaches Claude's API. Another company may use its own models or several providers. The concepts carry over; the parameter names don't. Read their docs before a technical round.
- **Lab-specific motivation.** Your "Why us?" paragraph must be rewritten from scratch (lesson 7). A values round built around one lab's mission doesn't transfer.
- **Domain.** Legal, government, customer service and data labelling each have their own vocabulary, constraints and customers. Learn the domain basics before you interview: who the customers are, what they're afraid of, what's regulated.

## Researching a new employer's process

Use C1's method:

1. **Start with the company's own pages:** careers site, job listing, any published interview guide. That's **Official**.
2. **Then candidate accounts.** Count independent sources, not repetitions (C1 lesson 4). Label what you find.
3. **Ask the recruiter** in the first call: stages, format, environment, AI rules for each round, and how level is decided (lesson 8).
4. **Ignore "leaked questions".** They're often wrong, they break confidentiality, and memorised answers show.

## Applying across companies without spreading thin

- **Keep one target role.** Applying for the same kind of role at several companies is efficient; applying for different kinds at once means several resumes and several stories (**General**).
- **Track every process** in one place: stage, next date, contact, what you submitted.
- **Reuse the facts, rewrite the motivation.** Your bullets and proof stories carry over; "why this company" never does.
