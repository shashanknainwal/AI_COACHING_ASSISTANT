questions:
  - q: "In this module, what does the label 'General' mean?"
    options:
      - "Common hiring practice and our judgement, not one company's stated process"
      - "A claim that several independent candidate accounts have reported about one lab"
      - "Something a lab states on its own careers page or in a posting"
      - "A rumour that appeared on one forum and hasn't been checked"
    answer: 0
    explain: "General marks industry practice and the course's judgement. Official, Reported and Anecdotal remain the labels for claims about a specific company's process, and the lab's own practice always wins."
  - q: "Your gap report shows a red on a must-have requirement. What's the best response?"
    options:
      - "Don't apply until every must-have is green"
      - "Leave the requirement off your notes so it doesn't worry you"
      - "Apply if the rest fits, and prepare one honest sentence about that gap"
      - "Add a course you plan to take as work evidence for it"
    answer: 2
    explain: "A red you know about is manageable; a red you haven't noticed is the problem. Postings describe an ideal hire, so prepare an honest answer rather than hiding or inflating it."
  - q: "Which resume bullet best follows the decision + metric + LLM relevance formula?"
    options:
      - "Leveraged cutting-edge GenAI to drive transformational efficiency gains across our support org"
      - "Responsible for the support chatbot and its evaluation and monitoring"
      - "Worked with the team on several AI projects that improved customer satisfaction"
      - "Wrote a 220-case eval suite for our support bot; it caught a refund regression pre-launch"
    answer: 3
    explain: "It names a personal decision, a sourced count and an outcome, and the work is directly LLM-relevant. The others are buzzwords, duties or team-level vagueness."
  - q: "Under Anthropic's official candidate AI guidance, how may you use Claude on your resume?"
    options:
      - "Ask Claude to draft it from your LinkedIn profile, then edit it into your voice"
      - "Write the first draft yourself, then use Claude to refine it and compare it with the posting"
      - "Let Claude estimate impact figures where you don't have the real numbers"
      - "Avoid Claude entirely, because the guidance bans any AI help at all on application materials"
    answer: 1
    explain: "The guidance asks for your own first draft and allows Claude to refine it, including finding what to highlight against the job description. Inventing experience or numbers is not allowed."
  - q: "You don't know the outcome of a project you led, and can't find it. What should the bullet use instead?"
    options:
      - "An honest scale, time or count figure, such as cases built or weeks to ship"
      - "A conservative estimate of the outcome, written so that it reads as a measured result"
      - "No numbers at all, since any figure might be questioned in the deep dive"
      - "The outcome a similar project at another company reported publicly"
    answer: 0
    explain: "Scale, time and count are honest substitutes for an outcome you never measured. An estimate presented as measured is exactly what a deep dive exposes."
  - q: "You want to contact an engineer at a lab you've never met. What's the best ask?"
    options:
      - "A referral for any open role, with your resume attached"
      - "A list of the questions they were asked when they interviewed"
      - "A 15-minute conversation about their team's work, easy to decline"
      - "An introduction to their manager so you can skip the recruiter"
    answer: 2
    explain: "A stranger can't honestly vouch for you, and asking for interview questions asks them to break confidentiality. A small, declinable ask about the work is respectful and more likely to get a reply."
  - q: "A recruiter asks for your compensation expectations in the first screen. Which reply follows the module's advice?"
    options:
      - "Give your single target number straight away so nobody wastes time"
      - "Quote a higher competing offer you expect to receive soon"
      - "Say compensation doesn't matter to you at all"
      - "Ask to understand the level and its band first, while staying cooperative"
    answer: 3
    explain: "The band usually depends on level, so naming a number first can anchor you badly. A polite, honest deflection keeps the conversation open; inventing offers or pretending pay doesn't matter does not."
  - q: "What is known about how Anthropic sets levels?"
    options:
      - "It is officially based on years of experience, in fixed bands"
      - "There's no reliable public source, so ask your recruiter how it's decided"
      - "Candidates choose their own level on the application form"
      - "It is reported to be set entirely by the online assessment score"
    answer: 1
    explain: "No reliable public source describes it. Commonly in the industry, level comes from scope and loop performance, but that's General; the recruiter is the source for a specific role."
  - q: "How do stock options differ from RSUs?"
    options:
      - "Options must be bought at a strike price to own the shares; RSUs are delivered as they vest"
      - "Options never expire, even years after you leave; RSUs must always be exercised within 90 days"
      - "RSUs are only offered by early-stage startups; options only by public companies"
      - "Options carry no tax consequences; RSUs are taxed only when you sell"
    answer: 0
    explain: "Options give the right to buy at a set price, which costs money and can trigger tax. RSUs are a promise of shares delivered on vesting. Exercise windows after leaving vary and are worth asking about."
  - q: "What's the right way to treat a tender offer at a private company?"
    options:
      - "As a guaranteed yearly chance to sell your vested shares, once the company has run one before"
      - "As proof that the equity is worth its latest paper valuation"
      - "As a right every employee has under standard vesting terms"
      - "As something the company runs at its discretion, so ask about it rather than assume"
    answer: 3
    explain: "Tender offers are set by the company: timing, eligibility and amounts. A past one doesn't promise a future one, so ask the recruiter and plan on cash covering your life."
  - q: "In the negotiation, the recruiter asks to see your competing offer. What should you do?"
    options:
      - "Refuse and repeat that they need to beat it by the end of the day"
      - "Mention a second offer to add pressure, even though you don't have one"
      - "Answer consistently and offer to share the relevant parts of the written offer"
      - "Inflate the other base salary a little, since they can't check it"
    answer: 2
    explain: "Honesty keeps the recruiter's trust and the flexibility you're asking for. A discovered bluff usually costs you movement, and sometimes the offer."
  - q: "A take-home brief says 'No AI coding tools'. Two hours in, you're behind. What should you do?"
    options:
      - "Use an assistant for the boilerplate only and mention it in the README"
      - "Cut scope, keep to the rules, and explain what you left out in the README"
      - "Keep going well past the time box so the result looks complete"
      - "Ask a friend to write the eval section while you finish the code"
    answer: 1
    explain: "Breaking a stated rule ends most processes regardless of quality, and Anthropic's guidance says take-homes are AI-free unless they say otherwise. Scoping down and saying what you cut is what reviewers want to see."
