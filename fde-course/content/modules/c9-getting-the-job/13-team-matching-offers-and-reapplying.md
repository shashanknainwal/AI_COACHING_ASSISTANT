> **By the end of this lesson** you'll be able to time your application against your prep, handle the quiet weeks of team matching, decide between offers on more than money, and plan a reapplication if the first attempt doesn't work out.

## Timing your application against your prep

| Fact | Label |
|---|---|
| At Anthropic, the online assessment is reported to come soon after the recruiter screen. | Reported (C1 lesson 5) |
| End-to-end timelines for Anthropic in third-party guides range from about three weeks to three months, with team matching the biggest variable. | Anecdotal (inconsistent guides; C1) |

The practical rule from C1 still holds: **don't apply before you've started preparing.** A good default (**General**):

1. Do C1–C3 and at least one timed C3 drill before you apply.
2. Apply when your resume, links and "Why us?" answer are ready (lessons 3–7).
3. Use the gap between the recruiter screen and the onsite for design, values and deep-dive prep (C4, C5, your track).
4. Ask the recruiter how much time you have before each stage. Asking for a short delay to prepare is common and usually fine; ask early, not the day before (**General**).

If you're running several processes, try to line them up so offers arrive close together. Applying to your first choice last, after practising elsewhere, is a common approach; just be honest with everyone about timelines (**General**).

## Team matching

| Fact | Label |
|---|---|
| At Anthropic, references and team matching after the onsite can add two to four weeks or more. | Reported |
| Passing the onsite isn't an offer at Anthropic: candidates describe a team-matching stage that can run long with little news. | Anecdotal |

What to do while you wait (**General**):

- **Treat team-match conversations as two-way interviews.** Ask what the team works on, who its customers are, what a first project might be, and how it measures success. C5 lesson 7 has questions for hiring managers.
- **Be clear about your preferences** (customer type, travel, technical area) without making them ultimatums.
- **Keep other processes moving** and tell the recruiter about any deadline. Silence during matching isn't necessarily a rejection.
- **Follow up politely** about once a week if you've heard nothing and you were told to expect news.

## References

Some companies ask for references after the onsite (Anthropic: **Reported**, C1). Prepare them before you need them (**General**):

- Pick two to four people who've seen your work closely, ideally a former manager and a peer.
- Ask permission first, tell them the role, and remind them of one or two projects that matter for it.
- Thank them afterwards and tell them the outcome.

## Choosing between offers

Money matters, and lesson 11 shows how to compare packages. Also weigh (**General**):

| Factor | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **The work** | Will I spend my days on the thing I chose in lesson 1? |
| **The team and manager** | Did I meet them? Would I learn from them? |
| **Level and scope** | Which offer sets me up best for the next two years? |
| **Risk** | How much of the value is cash, and how much is equity I can't sell? |
| **Logistics** | Travel, office days, location. Live with them for a year in your head. |
| **Mission** | Can I give an honest "why here" a year from now? (Your C1 answer is a test.) |

Once you've decided, tell the other companies promptly and politely. You may want to apply to them again one day (**General**).

## If it doesn't work out

Rejection is common at every stage, including for strong candidates. A few things help (**General**):

- **Ask for feedback.** Many companies don't share detailed feedback, often for legal reasons, but some recruiters will give a general steer. Ask once, politely: "Is there anything you can share that would help me next time?"
- **Write your own debrief within a day.** Which round, which question, what you'd do differently. Your own notes are often more useful than the company's feedback.
- **Ask when you can reapply.** Many companies have a waiting period before you can re-interview, usually measured in months, and policies differ (**General**). We found no public policy from Anthropic, OpenAI or Perplexity on this; the recruiter is the source.
- **Reapply with new evidence.** A reapplication is strongest when something has changed: a shipped project, the C8 capstone, a new skill that fills a red from lesson 2. Say what changed in the application or to the recruiter.
- **Keep the relationship.** A short thank-you note to the recruiter costs nothing. Recruiters move between companies and remember candidates who were gracious.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Don't apply before you've started preparing; ask for time between stages if you need it (**General**). The OA soon after the recruiter screen is **Reported**.
> - Team matching at Anthropic can add weeks (**Reported**) with little news (**Anecdotal**). Treat match calls as two-way interviews and keep other processes moving.
> - Line up references before you need them.
> - Choose offers on the work, team, level, risk and logistics as well as money, and decline others promptly.
> - After a rejection: ask for feedback once, write your own debrief, ask when you can reapply, and come back with new evidence.
