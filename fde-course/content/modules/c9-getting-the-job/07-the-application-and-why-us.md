> **By the end of this lesson** you'll be able to put together a complete application for one role, adapt your C1 "Why this lab?" draft for each company you apply to, and answer the other written questions without padding.

## What you already have

Module C1 lesson 2 had you write a "Why this lab?" answer of 200–400 words, graded on specificity, your own experience, honesty and economy. That's the hardest written piece. This lesson is about the rest of the application around it, and about reusing that work honestly across companies.

Quick recap of the labels from C1:

| Fact | Label |
|---|---|
| Anthropic's application includes a written answer to a question like "Why Anthropic?"; reports put it at 200–400 words. Treat it as your first interview. | Reported |
| Write the first draft yourself; Claude may help you refine it. | **Official** ([candidate AI guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance)) |
| Interviewers will ask about your experience and what motivates you. | **Official** (Anthropic [careers page](https://www.anthropic.com/careers), checked 2026-10-10) |

The second and third rows are why the written answer matters beyond the screen: whatever you write, expect to be asked about it. Keep a copy of everything you submit, and reread it before each round (**General**).

## The pieces of an application

Forms differ by company and change often, so this list is **General**. Read the live form before you write anything.

| Piece | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Resume** | Tailored to the role from lesson 1, top three bullets answering the posting's must-haves (lessons 3 and 4). |
| **Links** | LinkedIn, GitHub or the eval repo, one write-up (lesson 5). Check that every link works logged out. |
| **"Why us?" answer** | Your C1 draft, adapted (below). |
| **Other short answers** | Direct, specific, short. See below. |
| **Logistics** | Location, work authorisation, start date, travel. Answer accurately; these are filters, not essays. |
| **Cover letter** | If optional and you have a "Why us?" field, a cover letter usually adds little. If it's the only written space, use it for the "Why us?" content (**General**). |

### Which roles to apply for

Apply for the role your evidence supports (lesson 1). If two roles at the same company fit, check whether the form lets you list more than one, or ask a recruiter which one fits; applying to every open role at once tends to read as unfocused (**General**). We found no public rule from any lab on how many roles you may apply for at once.

## Adapting "Why us?" across companies

You'll probably apply to more than one company. The temptation is to write one answer and swap the name. C1's swap test catches that: if the answer still works with another company's name in it, it isn't specific enough.

What carries over and what doesn't:

| Part of your answer | Reuse? |
|---|---|
| Your experience and proof story | Yes. The facts about you don't change. Pick the story that best matches each company's role. |
| Why this role (the work itself) | Mostly. Adjust to the company's version of the role: pre-sales architecture, embedded engineering, a customer-facing advisor. |
| Why this company | **No.** Rewrite from scratch for each one, from something the company has actually published or built. Module C4 covers reading a lab's essays and policies critically. |
| Your honest question or tension | No. Each company's open questions are different. |

So the work per company is about one paragraph, plus a check that your proof story fits. That paragraph is where most of the score is.

For companies that aren't AI labs (lesson 14), "Why us?" still means the same thing: what this company does that matters to you, tied to something you've done.

## The other short answers

Forms commonly ask for one or two more short answers, such as something you've built or a hard problem you solved (**General**). The same rules apply.

- **Answer the question asked.** If it asks for "something you built", don't send your values.
- **One story, told well.** Decision, number, what you learned. C5's STAR stories are the raw material.
- **Stay inside the limit.** A word limit is part of the question. Using half of it well beats filling it.
- **No AI first drafts.** The guidance applies to all application writing (**Official**).

## A final check before you submit

1. Every link opens logged out.
2. Dates and titles match your LinkedIn.
3. The "Why us?" answer passes the swap test.
4. Every number in your resume has a source you could name in an interview.
5. You've saved a copy of everything you sent, with the posting text (postings get taken down).

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Anthropic's written "Why Anthropic?" answer is **Reported**; the first-draft rule and "interviewers ask about experience and motivation" are **Official**. Expect to be asked about whatever you write.
> - Apply for the role your evidence supports, not every role at once.
> - When adapting "Why us?", reuse the facts about you; rewrite the "why this company" paragraph from scratch every time.
> - Short answers: answer the question asked, one story, inside the limit, your own first draft.
> - Save everything you submit, including the posting.
