> **By the end of this lesson** you will be able to tell the FDE role apart from product SWE, solutions engineering and consulting, and spot the consultant trap.

At Brightline, Dana Ruiz will ask you to build things no other customer needs. Whether you say yes, and how, depends on which job you're actually doing.

## The comparison

| | Product SWE | Solutions / Sales Engineer | Consultant | **FDE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary goal** | Product for *all* customers | Win the deal | Deliver a statement of work | Make *this* customer successful, and improve the product |
| **Who sets the work** | Roadmap | Sales cycle | Contract | Customer outcome + company strategy |
| **Production code?** | Yes | Mostly demos | Sometimes | **Yes**, in the customer's environment |
| **Time horizon** | Quarters | Weeks (pre-sale) | Fixed project | Months, through and after go-live |
| **Success metric** | Features, reliability | Revenue closed | Delivered on time | Customer metric moved, expansion, product insight |

## The differences that matter

**vs. product SWE.** They build for everyone; you build what this customer needs by Friday, then hand them the pattern ("five customers built the same CSV importer").

**vs. solutions engineer.** SEs work before the contract, proving the product *can* solve the problem. FDEs work after, proving it *does*, in production. A demo can cut corners a deployment can't.

**vs. consultant.** Consultants deliver the contract and leave. FDEs represent a product company: the goal is renewal, expansion, and lessons that make the next deployment faster. A bespoke system only you understand is a failure, even if the customer is happy today.

> **The consultant trap:** if every deployment needs custom code that never reaches the product, your company becomes a services business. Ask, "How do I make this the last time anyone builds this by hand?"

## Where FDEs report

Engineering, customer success (risk: a ticket queue) or a dedicated deployment org (often healthiest). In interviews ask: *"How does deployment feedback reach the roadmap? Give me a recent example."*

> **Key takeaways**
> - FDEs own a customer outcome after the sale and write production code.
> - Unlike consultants, FDEs feed repeated custom work back into the product.
> - Ask how deployment feedback reaches the roadmap before you take an FDE job.
