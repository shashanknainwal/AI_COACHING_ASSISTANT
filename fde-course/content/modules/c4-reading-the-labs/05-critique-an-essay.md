This is the six-note framework from lesson 1, written up. It's also practice for the values round: a fair, specific critique is the kind of thinking those conversations reward.

## Pick one piece

Choose one, read it in full, and take your six notes before you write:

- Dario Amodei, [Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace) (October 2024)
- Dario Amodei, [The Urgency of Interpretability](https://darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability) (April 2025)
- Dario Amodei, [The Adolescence of Technology](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology) (January 2026); study guide in lesson 03
- Anthropic, [Responsible Scaling Policy version 3.0 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3) (February 2026)
- Anthropic, [Claude's constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution) (January 2026)

If you're targeting a different lab, you may pick a comparable published essay or policy from that lab. Name and link it in your summary.

## The rules

- **Your own words.** Paraphrase. Quote at most one short sentence, and only if the exact wording matters.
- **Write the first draft yourself.** Anthropic's [candidate AI guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance) asks applicants to write first drafts themselves and use Claude only to refine. Practise that habit here.
- **Attack the strongest version.** Your counterargument should answer your own steelman, not a weaker position.
- **Cite what you've read.** If you use a published critique, name it and link it. Don't cite something you know only from a headline.

## What a good answer sounds like

Calm, specific and fair. A reader at the lab should come away thinking "this person read it properly, disagrees with one thing for a good reason, and knows what it means for the job." They shouldn't come away thinking "this person is a fan" or "this person is a sceptic".

Submit for review when you're done. You'll get a score per rubric line, the strongest part of your draft, and the one change to make first. Revise and resubmit as often as you like.
